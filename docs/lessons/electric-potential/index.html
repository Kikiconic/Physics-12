Electric potential

Connect voltage, energy, and charge motion.

By the end, you can:

01Distinguish potential from potential energy

02Calculate point-charge potential

03Relate voltage change to energy and work

01

Energy per unit charge

Electric potential V is potential energy per coulomb. It is a property of a location in an electric field, measured in volts.

Unlike electric field, potential is a scalar. Potentials from several charges add algebraically, including the sign of each source charge.

V=UqV=\frac{U}{q}V=qU​
02

Potential of a point charge

Potential is positive near a positive source charge and negative near a negative source charge. The conventional zero is infinitely far away.

Potential decreases with 1/r, not 1/r². Keep the sign of Q when calculating.

V=kQrV=k\frac{Q}{r}V=krQ​
03

Voltage changes energy

Voltage is a difference in electric potential between two points. Moving a charge through a potential difference changes its electric potential energy.

The electric field does positive work when potential energy decreases.

ΔU=qΔVWfield=−ΔU\Delta U=q\Delta V\qquad W_{\mathrm{field}}=-\Delta UΔU=qΔVWfield​=−ΔU
Worked example

A proton moves through a potential difference of −250 V. Find its change in electric potential energy.

1ΔU=qΔV\Delta U=q\Delta VΔU=qΔV
2ΔU=(1.60×10−19)(−250)\Delta U=(1.60\times10^{-19})(-250)ΔU=(1.60×10−19)(−250)
3ΔU=−4.00×10−17 J\Delta U=-4.00\times10^{-17}\ \mathrm{J}ΔU=−4.00×10−17 J
Common mistake

Potential V is not a vector. Add potentials algebraically; do not resolve them into components.

Check your understanding

What is the electric potential 0.30 m from a −6.0 μC point charge?

Reveal solution

V = kQ/r = (8.99 × 10⁹)(−6.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.30) = −1.8 × 10⁵ V. The potential is negative because the source charge is negative.
